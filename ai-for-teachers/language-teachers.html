The same request, asked two ways

Ask for "a reading text" and you will usually get something too hard, too long and too generic. Here is the difference a few sentences make.

A first attempt

Write a reading text for my English class.

A prompt that works

I teach English to adult learners at B1 (CEFR). Write a 250-word reading text about working from home, using only the simple present and present perfect, with no idioms. Then write six comprehension questions: four true/false and two short-answer. List any words above B1 so I can add them to a glossary. Put the answer key at the end.

The second version says who you teach, what level, what to write and how it should come back. That is the habit we build in the first two sessions. It still needs checking: AI tends to drift above the level you ask for, so part of the work is checking and adjusting.

How we check the work

You know your students' level better than any tool does, so the checking stays with you. Session 8 is entirely about it.

  • Level: vocabulary and grammar matched to the CEFR level you teach, not the level the AI thinks it wrote
  • Natural English: dialogues that sound like real speakers, with no stiff textbook phrasing
  • Accuracy: facts in readings, and the English itself, including the examples in grammar exercises
  • Answer keys: gapped sentences with more than one correct answer, and keys that don't match the questions
  • Fit for your students: topics and examples they can relate to, and no cultural assumptions that don't suit your class

What you leave with

These are the targets we work towards. The exact numbers depend on your subject and how much material you bring.

  • 20 to 40 reusable lesson components, made from your own materials: quizzes, readings, worksheets, activities
  • 3 to 5 complete units or lessons, ready to teach
  • A repeatable process you can run on your own: write a prompt template, refine the draft, use it
  • The confidence to keep experimenting once we've finished

Ten one-hour sessions

Every session is a project built around your own materials. We start from what you need to make this week, not from a syllabus.

  1. 1–2

    Diagnostic and quick wins

    We look at what you make now and where the time goes, then produce your first useful pieces together.

  2. 3–8

    Deep application on your own materials

    Six project sessions: assessments and quizzes, readings and worksheets, interactive activities, and using Google's NotebookLM to work from your own source documents. You do the work, with me beside you.

  3. 9–10

    Integration and guardrails

    Fitting it into your week, and agreeing what you will and won't use AI for.

Full program details

AI drafts. You decide.

AI gets things wrong. It writes confident answers that are mistaken, drifts above the reading level you asked for, and produces quiz questions with two right answers. Checking the output is part of the method, not an extra.

The last two sessions are about guardrails: what to use AI for, what to leave alone, and what should never be typed into a tool, such as student names or personal details.

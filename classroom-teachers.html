The same request, asked two ways

Most disappointing AI output comes from a thin request. Here is the difference a few sentences make.

A first attempt

Make a worksheet about fractions.

A prompt that works

I teach Grade 6 maths. Create a one-page worksheet on adding fractions with unlike denominators. Include two worked examples, eight practice questions that start easy and get harder, and two word problems set in everyday situations. Keep the answers simple fractions. Put the answer key on a separate page.

The second version tells the AI who you teach, what to make and how it should come back. That is the habit we build in the first two sessions. It still needs checking: AI can get arithmetic wrong, including in its own answer key.

How we check the work

You are the one who stands in front of the class, so the checking stays with you. Session 8 is entirely about it.

  • Facts, dates and names, checked against a source you trust
  • Maths and answer keys, worked through by you
  • Reading level and vocabulary, matched to your students
  • Age-appropriateness, tone and bias
  • Fit with your curriculum or standards. AI doesn't know yours unless you tell it, and even then you check
